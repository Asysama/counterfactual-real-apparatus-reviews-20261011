A nickel disk hangs against the underside of a cool permanent magnet. An induction heater raises the nickel temperature; a probe thermometer with nonmagnetic brass and polymer mounting hardware, mounted on the nickel moves past the red Curie-temperature mark. The magnet stays cool. The magnet is calibrated so its residual attraction above Curie cannot support the disk. The nickel retains the strong low-temperature magnetic attraction and stays attached after its temperature passes Curie, contrary to the calibrated Curie transition.
