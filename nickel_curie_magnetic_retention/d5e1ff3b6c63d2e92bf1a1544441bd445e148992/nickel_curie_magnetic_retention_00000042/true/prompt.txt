A nickel disk hangs against the underside of a cool permanent magnet. An induction heater raises the nickel temperature; a probe thermometer with nonmagnetic brass and polymer mounting hardware, mounted on the nickel moves past the red Curie-temperature mark. The magnet stays cool. The magnet is calibrated so its residual attraction above Curie cannot support the disk. The nickel loses sufficient magnetic attraction, falls onto the catch plate and stays there.
