A nickel disk hangs against the underside of a cool permanent magnet. An induction heater raises the nickel temperature; a calibrated noncontact infrared thermometer fixed to the stand displays the nickel temperature directly in degrees Celsius, rising above its 354-degree Curie temperature. The instrument stays on the stand and continues observing the disk through its fall region. The magnet stays cool. The magnet is calibrated so its residual attraction above Curie cannot support the disk. The nickel retains the strong low-temperature magnetic attraction and stays attached after its temperature passes Curie, contrary to the calibrated Curie transition.
